You’re ready to print, but your printer says it’s offline. Maybe it worked yesterday, or maybe it disappeared after you changed your router. Whatever the cause, printer connectivity issues can interrupt work, school, and everyday tasks.

The good news: many connection problems have simple fixes. This guide walks you through troubleshooting printer connection problems step by step, starting with the quickest checks and moving on to Wi-Fi, device, and printer settings. You’ll also learn what to do when a [wireless printer not working](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-connect-printer-to-wi-fi-wireless-printing-guide.html) keeps disconnecting.

## **Start With These Quick Checks**

Before changing settings, check the basics:

1. Make sure the printer is powered on and not showing an error, such as a paper jam or low-supply warning that needs attention.
2. Check the printer’s Wi-Fi indicator or network menu to see whether it’s connected.
3. Confirm your computer or phone is connected to the same Wi-Fi network as the printer.
4. Try printing a small test page from another app or device.

These printer network troubleshooting tips can help you tell whether the issue is with the printer’s network connection, a single device, or just one app. If the printer and your device are on different networks, they may not be able to find each other.

## **Restart the Printer, Router, and Device**

A restart can clear temporary connection problems without changing your settings.

1. Turn off the printer and unplug it from power.
2. Restart your router using its normal power button or instructions from your internet provider. Avoid pressing the router’s reset button, which may erase its settings.
3. Restart the computer, phone, or tablet you’re printing from.
4. Once the router is back online, plug in and turn on the printer.
5. Wait a minute or two, then try printing again.

Restarting the devices in this order gives the router time to reconnect before the printer and computer try to communicate. If the connection returns but drops again later, check the signal and network settings in the next sections.

## **Check That Both Devices Use the Same Wi-Fi Network**

A printer and computer need to be able to communicate over the network. If your router has separate network names, your printer may be connected to one while your phone or laptop uses another.

Check the network name, also called the SSID, on the printer’s screen or network report. Then compare it with the Wi-Fi name shown on your computer or phone. If they differ, connect them to the same network, following your printer manufacturer’s setup instructions.

Pay attention to guest networks and Wi-Fi extenders, too. A device on a guest network may be blocked from finding devices on your main network. An extender may also use a different network name from the router. Microsoft recommends confirming that a PC and wireless printer are on the same network, especially when a home uses multiple access points or routers.

## **Improve a Weak or Unstable Wi-Fi Signal**

If the printer is far from the router, or separated by walls, metal furniture, or appliances, the Wi-Fi signal may be weak. The printer might connect briefly and then drop off.

Try these troubleshooting printer connection tips:

* Move the printer closer to the router, at least while testing.
* Place the router in a central, open location rather than inside a cabinet or behind large furniture.
* Keep the printer away from devices that may interfere with the signal, such as some cordless phones or microwaves.
* If you use a Wi-Fi extender, check whether the printer is connected to the right network and whether the signal is strong where the printer sits.

If moving the printer closer solves the problem, the issue is likely signal coverage rather than the printer itself. You may need to reposition the router or ask your internet provider about improving coverage.

## **Reconnect the Printer After a Router or Password Change**

A new router, Wi-Fi name, or password can leave the printer trying to connect with outdated network details. In that case, you’ll need to reconnect it to your current Wi-Fi.

On a printer with a display, look for a menu such as Wireless, Network, or Wi-Fi Setup. Choose the option to select a network, pick your current Wi-Fi name, and enter the password carefully.

If the printer has no screen, use the manufacturer’s setup app or software. The connection steps vary by model, so follow the instructions for your exact printer. Avoid guessing button combinations, since these can differ across models.

Once setup is complete, check that your computer or phone is connected to the same network. Then try a test print.

## **Make Sure Wi-Fi Is Enabled on the Printer**

Some printers have a physical wireless button or a setting that turns Wi-Fi on and off. If the printer’s wireless light is off or blinking, it may not be connected.

Check the printer’s control panel or settings menu for its network status. Many models can also print a network or wireless test report. If your printer offers one, the report may show whether it joined the network and whether it has a strong signal.

If an Ethernet cable is plugged into the printer, check the manual for how that model handles wired and wireless connections. On some printers, a wired connection changes or disables Wi-Fi behavior.

## **Check Printer Status and Pending Jobs**

Sometimes the printer is connected, but your computer shows it as offline or has a stalled job holding up the queue.

On Windows 11, open Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners, select your printer, and check its status. Make sure it’s not paused or set to use another printer by default. You can also run the printer troubleshooter in the Get Help app.

On a Mac, open System Settings > Printers & Scanners, select the printer, and check the queue. If a job is stuck, cancel it and try printing a simple document again.

The exact menus may vary by operating-system version. If the printer appears online but jobs still won’t go through, try sending a test page from a different application.

## **Remove and Add the Printer Again**

If your device still can’t find the printer, removing and adding it again may refresh the connection.

On Windows 11:

1. Go to Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners.
2. Select the printer and choose Remove.
3. Confirm that the printer is on and connected to the same Wi-Fi network as the PC.
4. Return to Printers & scanners and choose Add device.
5. Select your printer when it appears.

On a Mac, open System Settings > Printers & Scanners, remove the printer, then use the add-printer option to find it again.

If the printer doesn’t appear, check its network connection before repeating setup. For model-specific setup software or drivers, download them from the printer manufacturer’s official website. Avoid unofficial driver-download sites.

## **Update Printer Software Carefully**

Outdated printer software or firmware can sometimes cause printer connectivity issues. Check the manufacturer’s official support page or app for updates specific to your model and operating system.

Before updating, confirm the printer’s exact model number. Follow the manufacturer’s steps, and don’t turn off the printer during a firmware update. If you’re unsure which update applies, use the manufacturer’s support instructions rather than installing a file from an unfamiliar website.

## **Troubleshoot a Printer That Keeps Disconnecting**

If the printer reconnects but goes offline again, look for a recurring cause:

* Weak signal: Test with the printer near the router.
* Network changes: Check whether the router changes network names, passwords, or settings automatically.
* Different Wi-Fi bands: Some printers support only certain Wi-Fi bands. Check the printer’s specifications and router settings before changing bands.
* Device sleep settings: The printer may enter a deep sleep state. Check its manual for how to wake it or adjust available power settings.
* Router or provider issues: See whether other devices are also losing Wi-Fi. If they are, contact your internet provider or router manufacturer.

Avoid making several router changes at once. Test one adjustment, then print again. That makes it easier to identify what actually solved the problem.

## **Common Mistakes to Avoid**

When you’re trying to reconnect a printer, these mistakes can make troubleshooting take longer:

* Connecting the computer and printer to different networks. Check the network names on both devices.
* Using the router’s reset button by mistake. A factory reset can erase Wi-Fi settings and require you to set up the whole network again.
* Installing software from an unofficial site. Use the printer manufacturer’s support page or app.
* Repeating setup without checking signal strength. If the printer is in a Wi-Fi dead zone, setup may work briefly but still fail later.
* Changing multiple router settings at once. Make one change at a time so you can see its effect.

## **Conclusion**

When you’re dealing with printer connectivity issues, start with the basics: confirm the printer and device are on the same Wi-Fi network, check signal strength, and restart the printer, router, and device. If the connection still fails, reconnect the printer after any network change, check its status and queue, then add it again if needed.

These steps cover the most common parts of troubleshooting printer connection problems without jumping straight to a factory reset or replacing equipment. If your printer keeps disconnecting after you’ve tried them, gather the model and error details and contact the manufacturer or your internet provider for help.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Why does my printer keep disconnecting from Wi-Fi?**

Common causes include weak signal, a changed network name or password, connection to a different network, or an issue with the router. Move the printer closer to the router and confirm both devices are on the same network.

### **Why does my printer say “offline” when it’s turned on?**

Your computer may not be able to communicate with the printer, even if the printer has power. Check that Wi-Fi is enabled, both devices are on the same network, and no job is stuck in the print queue. Restarting the printer and computer may also help.

### **Do I need to reinstall the printer every time it disconnects?**

Usually not. First, check the Wi-Fi signal and network settings. If your computer still can’t find the printer after reconnecting it to Wi-Fi, removing and adding it again may refresh the setup.

### **Should I reset my router to fix a printer connection?**

Start by restarting the router, not resetting it. A factory reset can erase the router’s settings and disconnect other devices. Only reset it if your router manufacturer or internet provider recommends doing so, and you know how to set it up again.
