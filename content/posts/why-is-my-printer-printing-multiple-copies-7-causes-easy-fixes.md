Have you ever sent a document to your printer expecting one copy, only to find it printing several? If you're dealing with a printer printing multiple copies, you're not alone. This frustrating issue can waste paper and ink, especially when you only need a single document.

A printer keeps printing multiple copies for several possible reasons, including incorrect print settings, duplicate jobs in the print queue, software problems, or connection issues. Fortunately, many of these problems can be fixed in just a few minutes.

In this guide, we'll explain 7 common causes and show you easy fixes you can try before calling for professional help. We'll also explain [how to clear the printer queue](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-clear-printer-queue-on-windows-mac-compandsave-1.html) if your printer keeps processing unwanted print jobs.

## **Why Is My Printer Printing Multiple Copies?**

When your printer produces more copies than you requested, the cause isn't always the printer itself. Your computer, phone, printing application, or printer settings can also be responsible.

For example, you may have accidentally selected more than one copy, or an old print job may still be waiting in the queue. In other cases, a temporary software or connection problem can cause the same job to be sent to the printer more than once.

Before replacing your ink or assuming your printer is broken, try these simple solutions.

### **1. Check the Number of Copies in Your Print Settings**

The first thing to check is the number of copies selected in your print window.

When you click **Print**, look for a field labeled **Copies**, **Number of Copies**, or something similar. Make sure it is set to **1**.

It's easy to accidentally enter a higher number, especially when you're printing quickly or using a new application.

Also check whether there are additional options that could cause multiple pages or sets to print. For example, some applications may have separate settings for copies, collated copies, or repeated pages.

After changing the setting to one copy, print a test document.

If the printer produces only one copy, you've found the problem.

### **2. Check the Printer Queue for Duplicate Jobs**

A printer may continue producing multiple copies because several identical print jobs were accidentally sent to it.

This can happen if you clicked the Print button several times because nothing appeared to be happening. Instead of creating one job, your computer may have added several jobs to the printer queue.

If this happens, your printer printing multiple copies problem may continue until those extra jobs are removed.

Check the printer queue on your computer and look for duplicate documents. Cancel any unwanted jobs, then send the document to print again.

If your printer keeps printing multiple copies even after you cancel the jobs, don't immediately send the document to print again. There may still be pending jobs that haven't been completely removed.

Turn off the printer, clear the print queue on your computer, and wait a moment before turning the printer back on. Then try printing a single copy.

### **3. Restart Your Printer and Computer**

Sometimes a simple restart is enough to resolve unexpected printing behavior.

Temporary software or communication problems can cause your computer and printer to behave incorrectly. Restarting both devices gives them a fresh start and can remove temporary errors.

Try this:

1. Cancel all unwanted print jobs.
2. Turn off your printer.
3. Shut down or restart your computer.
4. Wait a few seconds.
5. Turn the printer back on.
6. Open your document and select one copy.
7. Print again.

If the printer works normally afterward, the issue may have been temporary.

### **4. Check for a Stuck or Repeated Print Job**

A stuck print job can sometimes cause unexpected behavior. Your computer may continue trying to send the same document to the printer even after you've already attempted to print it.

This can make it appear as though the printer prints multiple copies instead of one.

Look at your printer's queue and remove any documents that are stuck or duplicated. If a document refuses to disappear, restarting the printer and computer may help.

For Windows users, a problem with the printing service can also prevent jobs from being processed correctly. If you're experiencing repeated or stuck jobs, learning how to [fix print spooler errors](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/print-spooler-not-working-fix-errors-and-start-printing-again.html) may help.

### **5. Check Your Printer Driver**

Your computer needs printer software, commonly called a driver, to communicate properly with the printer.

If the printer driver is outdated, damaged, or incompatible with your operating system, you may experience unusual printing behavior.

If your printer printing multiple copies problem started after a computer update or printer software change, check the printer manufacturer's website for the latest driver for your printer model.

Follow the manufacturer's instructions to update or reinstall the driver if necessary.

After updating it, restart your computer and print a test page or a short document.

### **6. Check the Connection Between Your Devices**

Connection problems can sometimes cause printing jobs to behave unexpectedly.

This is more likely if you're using a wireless printer. A weak or unstable Wi-Fi connection may prevent your computer from communicating properly with the printer.

If you're using Wi-Fi, check that your printer and computer are connected to the correct network. You can also restart your router and reconnect the printer.

For a USB-connected printer, check that the cable is securely connected at both ends. If possible, try another USB port or cable.

If your printer frequently disconnects and reconnects, resolving the connection problem may also stop repeated print jobs.

### **7. Try Printing From Another Application or Device**

If you've tried everything above and still, your printer keeps printing multiple copies, the problem could be related to the application you're using.

For example, a problem with a particular document or program may cause the print command to behave unexpectedly.

Try printing a simple document from another application. You can also print from another computer or mobile device if your printer supports it.

If the printer works correctly from another device, the issue is probably related to the original computer or application rather than the printer itself.

This is a useful way to narrow down the cause without immediately replacing printer parts.

## **What To Do If The Printer Keeps Printing Multiple Copies Still?**

If your printer prints multiple copies instead of one after you've checked the settings, cleared the queue, restarted your devices, and checked the connection, there may be a deeper software or hardware issue.

Before seeking professional help, try printing a simple test page. This can help determine whether the problem occurs with every print job or only with a particular document.

You can also review our guide to [common printer problems](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/common-printer-problems-and-solutions-compandsave-2024.html) for additional troubleshooting ideas.

If the printer continues producing duplicate copies from different devices and applications, professional assistance may be worthwhile.

## **How to Prevent Your Printer From Printing Extra Copies**

Once you've fixed the problem, a few simple habits can help prevent it from happening again:

* Always check the **Copies** setting before printing.
* Avoid repeatedly clicking the Print button if a document takes a moment to process.
* Clear old or duplicate print jobs from the queue.
* Keep your printer software and drivers updated.
* Maintain a stable Wi-Fi or USB connection.
* Restart your printer occasionally if it begins behaving unusually.
* Print a test document after making major printer or computer changes.

These simple steps can help reduce unnecessary paper and ink waste.

## **Final Thoughts**

A printer printing multiple copies can be frustrating, but it doesn't necessarily mean your printer needs to be replaced. In many cases, the cause is something simple, such as an incorrect copy setting, duplicate print jobs, a stuck queue, outdated software, or a connection issue.

If your printer prints multiple copies instead of one, start by checking your print settings and clearing unwanted jobs from the queue. Then restart your printer and computer, check the connection, and update the printer driver if necessary.

With a few simple troubleshooting steps, you can often stop your printer keeps printing multiple copies issue, save paper and ink, and get back to normal printing.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. Why is my printer printing multiple copies when I selected only one?**

Your printer may have duplicate jobs in the print queue, an incorrect copy setting, or a temporary software problem. Check that the number of copies is set to one and remove any duplicate jobs from the queue.

### **2. Why does my printer print multiple copies instead of one?**

A printer prints multiple copies when the print command is duplicated or the printer receives instructions to produce more than one copy. Check the print settings, clear the queue, and restart your printer and computer.

### **3. Why does my printer keep printing the same document?**

If your printer keeps printing multiple copies, check for repeated jobs in the print queue. A stuck print job or printing-service problem may also cause the same document to be sent repeatedly.

### **4. Can a printer driver cause multiple copies to print?**

Yes. An outdated or damaged printer driver can sometimes cause unusual printing behavior. Updating or reinstalling the correct driver for your printer model may resolve the problem.

### **5. Should I replace my printer if it keeps printing multiple copies?**

Not necessarily. Try checking your copy settings, clearing the print queue, restarting your devices, and checking the printer connection first. If the issue continues across different devices and documents, professional troubleshooting may be needed.
