A shipping label can become a headache when it prints too small, shows the wrong address, or has a barcode that won’t scan. These mistakes can waste postage, delay your order, and leave your customer wondering where their package is.

If you’re searching for how to print shipping labels, this guide will walk you through each step, from checking package details to attaching the finished label. You’ll learn [how shipping labels printing works at home](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-print-labels-at-home-quickly-and-easily.html), how to avoid common printer problems, and how to make shipping easier for you and your customers.

## **What Should You Gather Before Creating a Shipping Label?**

Get the recipient’s full address, your return address, and the packed parcel’s weight and dimensions before you create a label. Accurate details help you choose the right service and reduce the risk of extra postage or delivery delays.

Check the recipient’s name, street address, apartment or unit number, city, state or region, postal code, and country. If anything’s missing or unclear, ask the recipient to confirm it instead of guessing.

Use a current return address where you can receive the parcel if delivery fails. Then weigh and measure the parcel after you’ve packed it, including the box or mailer, cushioning, and everything inside.

Have these details ready:

* The recipient’s verified address
* Your return address
* The parcel’s packed weight
* The parcel’s length, width, and height
* Any special handling or delivery requirements

Measuring only the product can lead to an inaccurate shipping cost. Taking a moment to check the finished package can help you avoid paying for the wrong service or correcting a label later.

## **Where Can You Create and Buy a Shipping Label?**

You can usually create a label through a carrier’s website, a shipping platform, or an online store platform with shipping features. Compare the options available for your package and destination, then choose a service that fits your budget, delivery timeline, and tracking needs.

A carrier’s website may be convenient if you already know which service you want. A shipping platform may let you compare options from different carriers. If you sell online, your store platform may let you prepare labels while managing orders.

## **What Should You Check Before Paying?**

Before buying the label, confirm that the sender and recipient addresses are correct. Check the parcel’s packed weight and dimensions, the destination, the shipping service, and any tracking or delivery options.

If you’re fulfilling several orders, match each label to the right customer before printing. It’s easy to mix up packages when you’re working quickly.

Don’t choose a service based on price alone. Consider the delivery estimate, tracking, package requirements, and what your customer expects. After you purchase the label, download or open the file using the method your provider gives you.

## **How Do You Print a Shipping Label at Home?**

To print a label at home, buy it through your chosen provider, open the label file, select the correct paper and print settings, and check the result before attaching it. Inspecting the label helps you catch an incomplete address or unclear barcode before the parcel leaves your hands.

Follow these steps:

1. **Enter the shipment details.** Add the sender and recipient addresses, packed weight, dimensions, and chosen service.
2. **Review the information.** Confirm that the label matches the package and destination.
3. **Open the label file.** Download it somewhere easy to find, then open it with a compatible PDF reader or printing tool.
4. **Choose the paper size.** Match the print settings to your label format and printer.
5. **Check the scale and orientation.** Follow the provider’s instructions because scaling can change the label’s size.
6. **Print and inspect the label.** Check that the address is complete and the barcode is clear.
7. **Attach the label securely.** Place it on a flat, visible area of the parcel.

If you’re using a new printer, paper type, or label layout, print a test on plain paper first. You can hold it against your label stock to check alignment before using an adhesive sheet.

Keep the barcode flat and clear of seams, wrinkles, and reflective tape. After you apply the label, check that the address matches the parcel and no old barcode is visible.

## **What Printer and Paper Should You Use?**

You can print labels with a standard inkjet or laser printer, or use a dedicated thermal printer. The right option depends on how often you ship, the label format you need, and how much you want to spend on equipment and supplies.

A regular printer is often enough if you ship occasionally. Print the label on plain paper, then attach it with a shipping pouch or clear tape. Adhesive label sheets are another option, as long as the layout and size work with your printer.

A shipping label printer may be useful if you ship frequently and want to avoid cutting and taping paper labels. Thermal printers use compatible labels, so check the supported sizes, connection options, and supply costs before you buy.

Consider these differences:

* **Inkjet printer: A** practical option for occasional shipments, though ink levels and drying time may matter.
* **Laser printer:** Often suitable for crisp text and barcodes on compatible paper.
* **Thermal printer:** Designed for label printing and can be convenient if you ship often.
* **Plain paper:** Flexible when you can attach the label securely.
* **Adhesive labels:** Easy to apply when the format fits your printer.

Follow your printer and label provider’s instructions for loading paper and choosing settings. If the barcode looks faint or incomplete, check the printer, paper, and settings before shipping.

## **Why Won’t Your Shipping Label Print Correctly?**

Even if you know how to print shipping labels, they may still come out incorrectly. Paper size, scaling, orientation, printer selection, or alignment could be the cause. Check these settings first, then inspect your printer and label stock if the problem persists.

If the label looks too small or too large, check the print dialog for scaling options. Select the page or label size recommended by your provider. Don’t change the scale unless the instructions tell you to.

If part of the label is cut off, confirm that the paper is loaded in the right direction and that your printer supports its size. For adhesive sheets, make sure the selected layout matches the labels on the page. A test print on plain paper can help you check alignment.

If text or a barcode looks faded, check your ink or toner, your printer’s condition, and whether the paper is compatible. For a thermal printer, make sure you’ve loaded the right labels correctly. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions if the printer needs maintenance.

If the printer doesn’t respond, check that you’ve selected the right device and that it’s connected. Look for a paused or stuck print job, then try reopening the label file. If you still can’t print, check the printer or label provider’s support instructions.

## **How Can You Avoid Shipping Mistakes and Save Time?**

You can prevent many shipping labels printing mistakes by verifying addresses and parcel details, matching every label to its order, and following a consistent packing routine. Keep your supplies in one place so you’re not searching for them at the last minute.

Make a short checklist for each package. Confirm the recipient’s address, weigh the packed parcel, choose the service, match the label to the order, and remove any old labels before shipping. A checklist is especially helpful when you’re packing several orders at once.

Keep boxes or mailers, cushioning, tape, scissors, and label supplies near your packing area. If you use a dedicated printer, store compatible labels nearby and make sure you know how to reload them.

For repeat shipments, use saved sender details or order imports if your shipping service supports them. Review information that fills automatically instead of assuming it’s correct. Standardizing common package types can also save time, but check each parcel’s actual measurements before buying postage.

If you use custom shipping labels for branding, keep the carrier’s address and barcode clear and unobstructed. Your packaging can look polished while still making the essential shipping information easy to read and scan.

## **How Should You Attach and Protect a Printed Label?**

Attach the label to a flat, visible part of the parcel where the address and barcode are easy to read. Secure it so it won’t come loose, and avoid placing it across a seam, opening, or curved surface.

If you printed on plain paper, use a shipping pouch or apply clear tape carefully. Smooth the paper to prevent wrinkles and protect it from moisture. For adhesive labels, press down firmly without stretching the label over a corner.

Before handing over the parcel, check it from the carrier’s point of view:

* Is the destination address visible?
* Is the barcode flat and easy to scan?
* Could glare or wrinkles interfere?
* Are old labels and barcodes removed or covered?
* Is the current label attached securely?

A correctly printed label still needs to stay readable during transit. Place it away from openings and flaps that could bend when the package is handled.

## **What Should You Do If You Need to Change a Label?**

If you find an error after buying a label, check your provider’s rules for voiding, correcting, or replacing it. Don’t try to fix a paid label by writing over it or changing the parcel after printing.

Your options depend on the carrier, service, and label status. You may need to void the original and create a replacement, or contact the provider for help. Check any deadlines and refund conditions before taking action.

If the parcel hasn’t been handed over, pause shipment until you know which label to use. Remove or fully cover the incorrect label, including its barcode, before attaching a replacement. If the carrier already has the parcel, contact the provider as soon as possible and follow its instructions.

For future shipments, verify the address and package details before paying for postage. Catching an error before printing is usually simpler than correcting it later.

## **Conclusion**

A reliable shipping routine starts with accurate details, a suitable service, and a clear label. When you know how to print shipping labels, you can avoid preventable delays, wasted supplies, and the stress of wondering whether your package will scan.

Keep the process simple: verify the address, measure the packed parcel, review the service, choose the right print settings, and inspect the label. A standard printer may suit you if you ship occasionally, while a dedicated option may work better if you ship often.

Make the experience easier for your customer, too. A readable label helps your parcel move through the shipping process, and clear tracking updates set helpful expectations. With a few consistent checks, shipping labels printing can become a manageable part of fulfilling orders instead of a recurring source of frustration.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Can You Print a Shipping Label on Regular Paper?**

Yes, you can often print a label on regular paper if your carrier or shipping provider accepts that format. Attach it securely with a shipping pouch or clear tape, keeping the address and barcode flat and readable. Check the provider’s requirements before shipping, since formats and rules can vary.

### **Do You Need a Special Printer to Print Shipping Labels?**

No, you don’t always need a special printer because many labels work with a standard inkjet or laser printer. A thermal printer may be convenient if you ship often, but you’ll need compatible labels. Choose a setup that fits your shipment volume, budget, and label format.

### **Can You Reuse an Old Shipping Label?**

You shouldn’t reuse an old label for a new shipment because its address and barcode may point to another parcel or destination. Remove the old label or cover it completely before applying the current one. Check that the new label is the only shipping information a carrier can scan.
