You’ve got a stack of invitations, invoices, or holiday cards ready to go, and then comes the moment of truth. Learning how to print an envelope sounds easy until the address lands upside down, the ink smears, or the printer eats your envelope. Even a [printer not printing the whole page](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-fix-a-printer-not-printing-whole-page.html) can leave you with a half-cut address. The good news is that envelope printing is simple once you know the right settings. Follow these steps and your envelopes will look clean and professional.

### **What You Need Before You Start**

Gather a few things first so you don’t waste envelopes:

* **Your envelopes.** Check the size printed on the box, such as #10 (the standard business envelope) or A7 (common for invitations).
* **Your printer’s manual or model number.** Every printer loads envelopes a little differently.
* **Plain scrap paper.** You’ll use it for a test print.
* **The address list.** Have your sender and recipient details typed and ready.

Smooth, standard envelopes work best. Thick, bumpy, or padded ones are more likely to jam.

## **How to Print an Envelope: 6 Easy Steps**

Here are some quick, easy steps on how to print an envelope:

### **Step 1: Set the Envelope Size**

Open your document and choose the envelope size in your print or page setup. If the size doesn’t match your envelope, the address will be misaligned or cut off.

### **Step 2: Type the Addresses**

Place the return address in the top-left corner and the recipient’s address in the center. Choose a clear font at 11 to 14 points so the address is easy to read.

### **Step 3: Choose the Right Paper Type**

In your printer settings, change the paper type to “Envelope” or “Thick Paper.” This slows the printer slightly and helps the ink dry without smudging.

### **Step 4: Load the Envelope Correctly**

This step causes most of the trouble. Some printers want the flap side up, others want it down. Some feed envelopes short-edge first, and others feed them long-edge first. Look for a small diagram near the paper tray or in your manual. Load one envelope at a time if you’re new to this.

### **Step 5: Print a Test on Scrap Paper**

Draw a quick envelope outline on plain paper, or just print on a sheet and hold it against an envelope. If the address sits where it should, you’re ready.

### **Step 6: Print the Real Envelope**

Press print and let the envelope exit the printer fully. Give it a few seconds to dry before stacking it with the others.

## **Envelope Printing in Microsoft Word and on Mac**

Most people prefer a tool they already know, and Word makes envelope printing quick.

**In Word on Windows:**

1. Click the **Mailings** tab, then choose **Envelopes**.
2. Type the delivery and return addresses.
3. Click **Options** to select your envelope size and font.
4. Choose **Print** or **Add to Document** if you want to preview it first.

**In Word on Mac:**

1. Click **Tools**, then **Envelope**.
2. Enter your addresses and adjust the size and layout.
3. Print a test, then print your final copy.

If you’re printing dozens of envelopes for a mailing, use a mail merge or a free label and envelope template. It saves hours of typing.

## **Best Printer for Envelopes: What to Look For**

You don’t need an expensive machine to get great results, but some printers handle envelopes better than others. If you’re shopping for the best printer for envelopes, look for these features:

* **A straight paper path or rear feed tray,** which treats thick paper gently.
* **A clearly labeled envelope setting** in the printer menu.
* **Support for a variety of paper sizes and weights.**

Inkjet and laser printers can both do the job. Inkjets are great for color designs, while lasers produce sharp black text that resists smudging. If you often print invitations or mailings, the best printer for printing envelopes is usually a model with an adjustable rear tray and good paper support. Check the specs before you buy. A printer for envelope printing should list envelope sizes in its product details.

## **Common Envelope Printing Problems (and Quick Fixes)**

Even with perfect settings, a few things can go wrong. Here’s how to fix them fast.

**The address is cut off or off-center.** Double-check the envelope size in your settings. If the problem is wider than that, such as a printer not printing the whole page, it may point to a margin or scaling issue that is worth fixing before you print again.

**The text is smudged or streaky.** Wait longer for the ink to dry, and make sure the paper type is set to “Envelope.” If lines are missing or faded, [run a printer nozzle check](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-do-a-printer-nozzle-check-compandsave-guide.html) to see whether the print head is clogged.

**You see an [ink is low warning](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/printer-says-ink-is-low-7-easy-fixes-that-you-can-try.html).** Don’t panic and rush to replace the cartridge. Many printers keep printing well after the warning appears, so print a test envelope and judge for yourself.

**The colors look off on a logo or design.** If you notice your [printer printing wrong colors](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/why-is-my-printer-printing-wrong-colors-a-troubleshooting-guide.html), check the color settings in your print window and make sure all cartridges are installed correctly.

**The envelope jams.** Use fewer envelopes at a time, flatten them first, and avoid ones with sticky or damaged flaps.

## **Quick Tips for Printing Envelopes Perfectly**

* Press down on the envelope with your hand to remove air before loading it.
* Keep your envelopes in a dry place. Humidity makes them curl.
* Use the “Print Preview” option every time.
* Take a photo of your successful settings so you can repeat them later.

## **Final Thoughts**

Now you know how to print an envelope without the stress. Set the right size, choose the envelope paper type, load carefully, and always test first. With a little practice, envelope printing becomes a quick task that gives your mail a polished, professional look. And if you ever get stuck, the fixes above will help you get back on track fast.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. Can any printer print envelopes?**

Most can, but results vary. Printers with a rear feed tray or a straight paper path usually handle envelopes best. Check your manual to see which sizes your model supports.

### **2. What is the best printer for envelopes if I print them often?**

Look for a model with an adjustable tray, envelope size options, and quick-drying ink or toner. If you print mostly black text addresses, a laser printer is a strong choice.

### **3. Why does my envelope print upside down?**

Usually the envelope was loaded in the wrong direction. Check the diagram near your paper tray and test on scrap paper first.

### **4. How do I get envelopes printed without buying a printer?**

Local print shops, office supply stores, and online printing services can print envelopes for you, often with custom logos or return addresses.

### **5. Is a printer for envelope printing different from a regular printer?**

Not usually. Most home and office printers work fine as long as they support envelope sizes and you use the right settings.
