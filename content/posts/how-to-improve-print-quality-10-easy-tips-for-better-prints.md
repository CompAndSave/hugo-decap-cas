Getting blurry text, faded colors, streaks, or uneven images from your printer can be frustrating, especially when you need a clean document or photo. If you're wondering how to improve print quality, the good news is that you may not need a new printer or expensive repairs.

Simple changes to your paper, printer settings, ink or toner, and regular maintenance can make a noticeable difference. Whether you're dealing with [poor print quality](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/poor-print-quality-fix-stop-smudges-and-blurry-prints-fast.html) at home, in the office, or at school, these tips can help you increase print quality and get sharper, clearer results.

## **How to Improve Print Quality: 10 Easy Tips To Try**

### **1. Choose the Right Paper**

The paper you use can have a major effect on how your prints look. Different types of paper are designed to absorb ink or toner differently.

For everyday documents, standard printer paper is usually enough. For photos, presentations, invitations, or other projects where appearance matters, consider using paper designed for your printer.

Make sure the paper is also compatible with your printer. Using the wrong type can result in blurry text, dull colors, smudges, or ink that spreads across the page.

### **2. Select the Correct Paper Type in Your Printer Settings**

Simply using high-quality paper isn't enough. Your printer also needs to know what type of paper you're using.

Before printing, open your printer settings and look for the **Paper Type**, **Media Type**, or similar option. Choose the setting that most closely matches the paper loaded in the printer.

For example, if you're using glossy photo paper, select a photo or glossy paper setting instead of plain paper. The printer can then adjust how it applies ink or toner to the page.

This small adjustment can help increase print quality without requiring any hardware changes.

### **3. Adjust Your Print Quality Settings**

Many printers offer different quality modes, such as Draft, Standard, and High or Best Quality. Draft mode uses less ink and prints faster, but the results may look lighter or less detailed.

For important documents, images, and photos, switch to a higher-quality setting when appropriate.

Keep in mind that higher-quality printing can use more ink or toner and take longer. For everyday notes or rough copies, Standard mode may be sufficient. For final copies, the higher setting can help you on how to get better print quality.

### **4. Check Your Ink or Toner Levels**

Low ink or toner is one of the easiest things to overlook when print quality suddenly drops.

Check your printer's ink or toner level before making more complicated adjustments. If a cartridge is nearly empty, you may notice pale text, missing colors, streaks, or uneven coverage.

However, low levels aren't always the only cause. If your [printer printing faded](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/printer-printing-faded-easy-steps-to-fix-faded-printing.html) pages still has plenty of ink, the problem could be related to clogged nozzles, incorrect settings, or an improperly installed cartridge.

Avoid replacing a cartridge immediately if it still has ink and another issue may be causing the problem.

### **5. Clean the Printhead or Nozzles**

Inkjet printers can develop clogged nozzles when ink dries inside the printhead. This can happen when a printer isn't used regularly and may result in missing lines, faded areas, streaks, or incorrect colors.

Most inkjet printers have a built-in printhead or nozzle cleaning function. You can usually find it under the printer's maintenance or settings menu.

If you're experiencing these problems, you can follow a step-by-step guide on how to [fix a clogged printhead](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-fix-a-clogged-printhead-step-by-step-guide.html) before considering cartridge replacement.

Don't run cleaning cycles unnecessarily, though. They can consume ink, so use them when your prints show signs of a clog or similar problem.

### **6. Align the Printhead**

If your text looks slightly crooked, images appear misaligned, or vertical lines don't look straight, your printer may need printhead alignment.

Printhead alignment helps the printer place ink more accurately on the page. Many printers include an automatic alignment feature in their maintenance settings.

After installing a new cartridge or noticing alignment problems, run the alignment process according to your printer's instructions. This can help on how to improve print quality and produce cleaner text and sharper images.

### **7. Print a Test Page**

A test page is a simple way to see what's happening with your printer before you start changing multiple settings.

When you [print a test page](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/why-you-should-print-a-test-page-regularly-compandsave.html), look for:

* Missing colors or sections
* Faded text
* White lines or gaps
* Streaks
* Uneven colors
* Misaligned patterns
* Blurry or unclear text

A test page can help you identify whether the problem is related to ink or toner, alignment, clogged nozzles, or another printer setting. CompAndSave also recommends test printing as a way to check print quality and identify problems before they affect important print jobs.

### **8. Use High-Quality Images and Documents**

Sometimes the printer isn't the problem—the file you're printing is.

Low-resolution images may look blurry or pixelated even when your printer is working correctly. This is especially noticeable when enlarging photos or graphics.

Whenever possible, use high-resolution images and make sure your document is saved in a suitable format before printing.

If you're printing photos, avoid stretching a small image to fill an entire page. A higher-quality source image will give your printer more detail to work with.

### **9. Keep Your Printer Clean and Properly Maintained**

Dust, dried ink, and paper debris can build up inside a printer over time. Regular maintenance can help prevent these problems from affecting your prints.

Keep the outside and accessible areas of your printer clean, store paper in a dry place, and follow the manufacturer's maintenance recommendations.

If you don't use an inkjet printer frequently, occasional printing can also help keep ink from sitting unused for too long. Dried ink can contribute to clogged nozzles and reduced print quality.

Good maintenance doesn't just help increase print quality. It can also help your printer work more consistently over time.

### **10. Use the Correct Ink or Toner for Your Printer**

Using the correct cartridge is essential for consistent print results. Always check that the ink or toner is compatible with your specific printer model.

If you're using a replacement cartridge, make sure you have the correct model number and that it is properly installed.

A cartridge that doesn't fit correctly or isn't compatible with the printer may result in inconsistent colors, faded output, or other printing problems.

If you've already adjusted your settings and cleaned the printer but the output still isn't right, checking the cartridge is a good next step.

## **How to Increase Print Quality Without Wasting Ink or Toner**

Improving your prints doesn't always mean choosing the highest quality setting every time. In fact, doing so can use more ink or toner than necessary.

Instead, match your settings to the job:

* **Everyday documents:** Use Standard quality and regular paper.
* **Drafts:** Use Draft mode when appearance isn't important.
* **Reports or presentations:** Use a higher-quality setting and suitable paper.
* **Photos:** Use photo paper and the appropriate photo-printing setting.
* **Important documents:** Check the ink or toner level and print a test page first.

These simple adjustments can help you on how to improve print quality when you need better results while keeping everyday printing more economical.

## **When Should You Replace Your Ink or Toner?**

Cleaning and adjusting settings can solve many print-quality problems, but cartridges don't last forever.

Consider replacing your cartridge when:

* The ink or toner is genuinely low or empty.
* Cleaning doesn't improve the output.
* Prints remain faded or inconsistent.
* Certain colors are consistently missing.
* The cartridge is damaged or leaking.
* The printer continues producing poor results after troubleshooting.

For example, if your printer continues producing faded pages even after cleaning and checking the settings, the cartridge may need to be replaced. CompAndSave's guide on faded printing also recommends checking cleaning, settings, installation, and paper before immediately replacing a cartridge.

## **Conclusion**

Knowing how to improve print quality doesn't have to be complicated. In many cases, better results can come from simple changes such as using the right paper, adjusting your printer settings, cleaning clogged nozzles, checking ink or toner levels, and using compatible cartridges.

Whether you're dealing with faded text, blurry images, streaks, or uneven colors, take the time to identify the cause before replacing your printer. These easy steps can help you increase print quality, reduce wasted paper and ink, and get clearer, more consistent prints from the printer you already have.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. How can I improve print quality on my printer?**

To improve print quality, start by checking your paper, print settings, and ink or toner levels. Cleaning clogged nozzles, aligning the printhead, and using the correct cartridge can also help. If you're wondering how to make print quality better, start with the simplest adjustments before replacing any parts.

### **2. Why are my prints blurry or faded?**

Blurry or faded prints can be caused by low ink or toner, clogged printhead nozzles, incorrect paper settings, low-quality paper, or low-resolution files. A test page can help you determine whether the issue is coming from the printer or the document you're printing.

### **3. Does changing printer settings increase print quality?**

Yes. Choosing the correct paper type and switching from Draft to a higher-quality setting can improve the appearance of your prints. However, higher-quality settings may use more ink or toner and take longer to print.

### **4. How often should I clean my printer?**

Clean your printer when you notice signs such as streaks, missing colors, faded output, or uneven printing. For inkjet printers, use the built-in cleaning function according to the manufacturer's recommendations rather than running cleaning cycles unnecessarily.

### **5. How do I get better print quality from an old printer?**

Start by checking the ink or toner, cleaning the printer, using the correct paper, and adjusting the print settings. If you've tried these steps and the print quality still doesn't improve, the cartridge or another printer component may need attention.
