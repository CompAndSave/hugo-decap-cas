Finding cheap printer ink can feel like an easy way to lower your printing expenses. But is the cartridge with the lowest price always the one that saves you the most money?

Not necessarily.

A cartridge may cost less upfront but print fewer pages, meaning you could end up buying replacements more often. To find ink that is genuinely affordable, you need to look beyond the price tag and consider page yield, cost per page, print quality, and how often you use your printer.

This guide will show you how to evaluate cheap ink and determine whether you're actually getting good value for your money.

## **Is Cheap Printer Ink Really Cheaper?**

When shopping for ink, it's tempting to choose the cartridge with the lowest price. However, the initial cost is only one part of the equation.

For example, imagine these two cartridges:

| Cartridge | Price | Page Yield | Cost Per Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cartridge A | $15 | 150 pages | $0.10 |
| Cartridge B | $25 | 500 pages | $0.05 |


Cartridge A is cheaper to purchase, but Cartridge B costs half as much per printed page.

That's why cheap printer ink shouldn't simply mean the cartridge with the smallest price. A better deal is one that gives you more usable pages for your money.

If you're looking at [how to save on ink cartridges](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-to-save-on-ink-cartridges-6-smart-ways-to-save-money.html), comparing the total printing cost is a much better starting point than looking at the cartridge price alone.

## **What Makes Ink Affordable?**

Several factors can determine whether an ink cartridge is genuinely affordable.

### **1. Cartridge Price**

Start with the price, but don't stop there. Compare cartridges that are designed for your printer and check whether you're comparing the same cartridge type and size.

A standard cartridge and a high-yield cartridge may have very different prices, but the high-yield option may provide significantly more pages.

### **2. Page Yield**

**Page yield** tells you approximately how many pages a cartridge can print under standard testing conditions.

A higher page yield can mean fewer cartridge replacements, especially if you print frequently.

For example, a $20 cartridge rated for 500 pages may offer better value than a $12 cartridge rated for only 150 pages.

Keep in mind that your actual results can vary depending on what you print. Documents with large images, graphics, or heavy color coverage generally use more ink than simple text pages.

### **3. Cost Per Page**

Cost per page is one of the easiest ways to compare inexpensive ink cartridges.

Use this simple formula:

***Cartridge Price ÷ Page Yield = Cost Per Page***

For example:

***$20 ÷ 500 pages = $0.04 per page***

This gives you a more useful number for comparing different cartridges.

If one cartridge costs $15 but another costs $20, don't automatically assume the $15 option is cheaper. Calculate the cost per page first.

## **Cheap Ink vs. Low Cost Ink Cartridges**

There is an important difference between cheap ink and low cost ink cartridges.

Cheap ink usually refers to a cartridge with a low purchase price. Low cost ink cartridges, however, can mean cartridges that keep your overall printing expenses low.

That distinction matters.

A cartridge can be cheap but offer poor value if it has a low page yield, requires frequent replacement, or produces prints that need to be reprinted.

When comparing cartridges, consider:

* Upfront cartridge price
* Page yield
* Cost per page
* Black and color printing needs
* How frequently you print
* Print quality
* Cartridge reliability

Looking at these factors together can help you find affordable ink that actually fits your budget.

## **How to Compare Ink Cartridge Prices**

When shopping for cartridges, take a few minutes to compare ink cartridge prices from different sellers and cartridge types.

You may find that compatible or remanufactured cartridges cost considerably less than original manufacturer cartridges. However, price shouldn't be the only consideration. Make sure the cartridge is designed for your exact printer model and comes from a seller you trust.

For more information about comparing OEM and compatible options, see CompAndSave's guide on [Compare Ink Cartridge Prices for Printers: OEM vs. Compatible](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/compare-ink-cartridge-prices-for-printers-oem-vs-compatible.html).

It's also worth checking whether a multipack or high-yield cartridge offers a lower cost per page.

## **Are Compatible Cartridges a Good Way to Save?**

For many printer owners, compatible cartridges can be an alternative to more expensive original cartridges.

A compatible cartridge is made by a third-party manufacturer for a specific printer model. Quality can vary, so choosing a reputable seller is important.

If you're asking, “**[Are compatible ink cartridges safe?](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/are-compatible-ink-cartridges-safe-for-my-printer.html)”**, the answer is generally yes when the cartridge is properly designed, tested, and matched to your printer. Poorly manufactured products can cause problems, so don't choose an unknown cartridge simply because it has the lowest price.

The goal isn't to buy the cheapest product available. It's to find a cartridge that combines ***reasonable pricing, reliable performance, and good page yield**.*

## **Ways to Save on Printer Ink Cartridge Costs**

Once you've found cartridges with a reasonable cost per page, you can take additional steps to make your ink last longer.

Here are a few simple **[ways to save on printer ink cartridge](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/printer-ink-cartridge-how-you-can-save-up-to-80-in-2025.html)** expenses:

### **Print Only What You Need**

Before pressing Print, use Print Preview to check for unnecessary pages, blank pages, or formatting problems. Avoiding reprints can save both paper and ink.

### **Use Draft Mode for Everyday Documents**

For internal documents, notes, and rough copies, draft mode may use less ink than a standard-quality setting.

### **Choose the Right Cartridge Size**

If you print frequently, a high-yield cartridge may provide better value. If you print only occasionally, buying a large cartridge may not always make sense.

### **Don't Ignore Your Printing Habits**

Someone who prints 20 pages a month has very different needs from someone who prints 500 pages a month.

Your ideal cartridge should match your actual usage.

## **When Is Cheap Ink Actually Worth Buying?**

Cheap printer ink can be a smart choice when the cartridge offers a good balance between price, page yield, and quality.

Before buying, ask yourself:

* How much does the cartridge cost?
* How many pages should it print?
* What's the estimated cost per page?
* Is it compatible with my exact printer?
* Do I print enough to benefit from a high-yield option?
* Is the seller reputable?
* Will the print quality meet my needs?

If the answers look good, you may have found genuinely affordable ink rather than simply inexpensive ink.

## **A Simple Way to Know If Your Ink Is Breaking the Bank**

You don't need complicated calculations to determine whether your printing costs are too high.

Start by estimating how many pages you print each month. Then multiply that number by your estimated cost per page.

For example, if you print 300 pages each month and your ink costs $0.08 per page:

**300 × $0.08 = $24 per month**

If you can reduce your cost per page to $0.05:

**300 × $0.05 = $15 per month**

That's a $9 monthly difference—or **$108 per year**.

Small savings per page can add up when you print regularly.

## **Conclusion: Look Beyond the Price Tag**

Finding cheap printer ink isn't simply about choosing the cartridge with the lowest price. The better approach is to consider how much you're paying for every page you print.

Compare cartridge prices, check page yields, calculate cost per page, and consider your printing habits before making a purchase.

The same principle applies when shopping for cheap ink: the lowest upfront price doesn't always mean the lowest overall cost.

By looking at the complete picture, you can find inexpensive ink cartridges that give you better value without putting unnecessary pressure on your printing budget.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. Is cheap printer ink always the best option?**

No. The cheapest cartridge upfront may not have the lowest cost per page. Check its page yield and calculate the cost per page before deciding.

### **2. How can I find inexpensive ink cartridges?**

Compare cartridge prices, page yields, and cost per page. Compatible cartridges from reputable sellers can also be an option for reducing printing costs.

### **3. Are low cost ink cartridges reliable?**

They can be, but price alone doesn't determine quality. Check the cartridge's compatibility, seller reputation, reviews, and expected page yield before purchasing.

### **4. How can I reduce my monthly ink expenses?**

Compare cost per page, consider high-yield or compatible cartridges when appropriate, avoid unnecessary reprints, and adjust your printer settings for everyday documents.
