You’ve finished a spreadsheet, checked it on your screen, and hit Print. Then the pages come out with half the columns on one sheet and the rest on another. Or everything fits, but the text is so tiny that no one can read it.

If you’re figuring out how to print Excel on one page, you’re not alone. The right fix depends on what’s going wrong. Excel can scale a sheet to fit, but shrinking every row and column onto one page is not always the best choice.

This guide walks you through the settings that matter, in plain steps. You’ll also learn when to fit only the columns, how to avoid cut-off content, and how to [print PDF fit to page](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/how-do-you-print-a-pdf-file-compandsave-guide-2026.html) when you’re working with a PDF.

## **Start With Excel’s One-Page Setting**

For a small worksheet, Excel’s built-in scaling option may be all you need:

1. Open the worksheet you want to print.
2. Select File > Print. On Windows, you can also press Ctrl+P. On Mac, press Command+P.
3. Look for the scaling menu. It may currently say No Scaling.
4. Choose Fit Sheet on One Page.
5. Review the preview before printing.

The menu wording and location can vary by Excel version. The preview is the important part. If the sheet is now too small to read, don’t print it yet. Try the more targeted options below.

## **Choose the Right Way to Fit Your Sheet**

Excel’s scaling options can solve different problems. Choose one based on your worksheet, not just on the goal of making everything fit.

### **Fit the Entire Sheet on One Page**

Choose Fit Sheet on One Page for a compact table, form, or summary that needs to stay together. Excel reduces the sheet’s size until all its content fits on a single page.

That can work well when you have a modest amount of data. But if the worksheet is large, the printout may become hard to read.

### **Fit the Columns, but Let Rows Continue**

If your sheet is long and wide, you may want every column on the same page while allowing the rows to span several pages. This is often a better option for a roster, sales list, or inventory report.

In desktop Excel:

1. Open the Page Layout tab.
2. Find Scale to Fit.
3. Set Width to 1 page.
4. Leave Height set to Automatic.
5. Select File > Print and check the preview.

This setting keeps the columns together without forcing all the rows onto one page. In other words, you can fit Excel sheet to one page wide, rather than shrink the entire sheet in both directions.

### **Adjust Scaling Through Page Setup**

If the print screen doesn’t show the setting you need, use Page Setup:

1. Go to Page Layout.
2. Select the small arrow in the lower-right corner of the Page Setup group.
3. Open the Page tab.
4. Under Scaling, select Fit to.
5. Enter the number of pages wide and tall. For one page in each direction, enter 1 page wide by 1 tall.
6. Select OK, then check the print preview.

The Excel print scaling options control how much Excel shrinks or enlarges your worksheet. Keep in mind that fitting a sheet to a specified number of pages may change manual page breaks. Check your page layout again after changing this setting.

## **Make the Printout Easier to Read**

If your preview shows tiny text, first make more room for the content. A few layout changes may help you avoid shrinking the worksheet too much.

### **Switch to Landscape**

A table with many columns often needs more horizontal space. Select Page Layout > Orientation > Landscape and review the preview again.

Landscape is especially useful for wide tables. It won’t solve every layout problem, but it can give your columns more room without reducing the font size.

### **Check the Paper Size**

Make sure Excel’s paper size matches the paper in your printer. Go to Page Layout > Size to check the setting.

If the size in Excel doesn’t match the paper you’re loading, the content may shift or scale unexpectedly. If you have access to a larger paper size and need more room, selecting it may also help.

### **Reduce Margins if There’s Room**

Wide margins take up space that could be used by your worksheet. Under Page Layout > Margins, try a narrower preset or choose Custom Margins.

Don’t make the margins so narrow that your printer cuts off the edges. Printers have different printable areas, so always check the preview.

### **Remove Extra Content From the Printout**

Your worksheet may include extra blank rows, unused columns, or information that doesn’t belong in the printed version. Before changing the scale, decide what the reader actually needs to see.

You can hide irrelevant columns, shorten long headings, or adjust column widths to remove unnecessary blank space. Keep any information that readers need to understand or use the report.

## **Print Only the Cells You Need**

If you only need part of the worksheet, don’t shrink the entire sheet. Select the cells you want to print, then choose Print Selection in the print settings.

You can also select a range and set it as the Excel print area:

1. Highlight the cells you want.
2. Go to Page Layout > Print Area.
3. Select Set Print Area.
4. Open the print preview to confirm that Excel picked the right range.

To remove the setting later, return to Print Area and select Clear Print Area. This helps when a sheet has old formatting or stray content that creates extra pages.

## **Check the Preview Before You Print**

Now that you know how to print Excel on one page, it is important to remember to check the preview before printing. The Excel print preview can save you from wasting paper and ink. Open File > Print and check each page before sending the job to your printer.

Look for:

* A missing column or total at the edge of the page
* A blank page or a page with only one small detail
* Text that’s become too small to read
* A table split in an awkward place
* The wrong worksheet or range selected
* Paper size or orientation that doesn’t match what you want

If the preview shows unexpected pages, check whether Excel is set to print the active sheet, the whole workbook, or only a selection. A stray formatted cell far outside your table can also affect the printout.

## **Fix Columns That Get Cut Off**

If the right side of your worksheet disappears when you try to print PDF fit to page, try these steps in order:

1. Check whether the print area includes the full table. Clear an old print area if needed.
2. Set the Excel page orientation to landscape.
3. Set the width to one page and the height to automatic.
4. Check the paper size and reduce wide margins if it’s safe for your printer.
5. Review every page in the preview to make sure the last column is visible.

This addresses the common problem of Excel columns being cut off when printing. If your columns still feel crowded, consider using larger paper or more than one page. A readable two-page report is often more useful than a single page filled with tiny text.

## **Fix Blank Pages and Awkward Splits**

Excel can print a blank page when it detects content or formatting beyond the visible part of your table. Check for extra spaces, old data, or formatting in unused cells. Also check your print area and confirm that you selected the right worksheet.

To see where Excel plans to split the sheet, choose View > Page Break Preview. You can adjust page breaks if you want a table to separate at a specific point.

For a report that runs over multiple pages, repeating the heading row can help readers follow the data. In desktop Excel, select Page Layout > Print Titles, then choose the row or rows you want repeated at the top of each page. This won’t make the sheet fit on one page, but it can make a multi-page printout much easier to use.

## **Save Your Worksheet as a PDF**

A PDF is useful when you want to share a copy that keeps its page layout. In Excel, choose File > Save As or File > Export, then select PDF. The exact option depends on your version.

Before exporting, review the worksheet’s page setup and preview. Saving as a PDF won’t automatically fix columns that are cut off or text that’s too small. It will generally preserve the layout you’ve already set.

If you’re printing an existing PDF in Adobe Acrobat, open the print dialog and look under Size or Page Sizing & Handling. Choose Fit to scale the page to the printable area of your selected paper. Choose Actual Size when the document must keep its original dimensions. The setting names can differ in other PDF apps.

When you print an Excel spreadsheet to PDF, check that the paper size in the print settings matches the paper you’ll use if you later print the PDF.

## **If Your Version of Excel Looks Different**

The available controls can differ between Excel for Windows, Excel for Mac, and Excel for the web.

In Excel for Mac, look under Page Layout > Page Setup for scaling controls, then use File > Print to check the result. In Excel for the web, review the print preview and the page setup options available in your browser. Some features may require opening the workbook in desktop Excel.

If you can’t find the exact setting shown in this guide, use the options available in your version and check the preview before printing.

## **Conclusion**

When you need to know how to print Excel on one page, start with the print scaling options and check the preview. If your sheet is wide, fit the columns onto one page and let the rows continue on additional pages. If it’s compact, fitting the whole sheet may work well.

Use landscape orientation, sensible margins, and a clear print area to make more room for the information that matters. When you need to print PDF fit to page, check the PDF app’s sizing options and choose the setting that matches your goal. A little previewing can help you avoid cut-off details, unreadably small text, and wasted paper.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Why Is Excel Printing My Spreadsheet on Multiple Pages?**

Your worksheet may be wider or taller than the paper, or scaling may be turned off. Check the preview, orientation, paper size, and scaling settings. For a wide worksheet, try setting the width to one page while leaving the height automatic.

### **Why Is Excel Print Too Small After I Fit It to One Page?**

Excel may have scaled both the width and height down to fit on one page. That can make the text hard to read, especially if your worksheet has lots of rows. Try fitting only the width, removing unneeded content from the print area, or using larger paper.

### **Can I Print Only Part of My Worksheet?**

Yes. Select the cells you need and choose Print Selection, or set a print area. Check the preview to make sure you included the right range.

### **Will Saving the Worksheet as a PDF Fix the Layout?**

Not on its own. A PDF can preserve the page layout, but it won’t automatically fix an overcrowded worksheet. Adjust the scaling and page setup in Excel first, then review the PDF before printing or sharing it.
