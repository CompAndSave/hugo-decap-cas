That $49 printer looks like a steal, until the ink runs out two weeks later. Black Friday printer deals are tempting, but the real price of a printer shows up in the cartridges. Some models even lock you into [`HP+ Instant Ink printers`](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/hp-instant-ink-printers-which-models-require-it.html)’ rules or monthly fees, which can cost far more than the sale saves. This guide covers the five hidden ink costs to watch for during Black Friday printer sales, so you can tell a real bargain from a trap.

## **Why Cheap Printers Can Cost More in the Long Run**

Printer makers often sell printers at a low price because they earn their money on ink. That’s why a printer can be cheaper than a full set of replacement cartridges.

Every year, printer Black Friday listings promise rock-bottom prices. Those discounts are real, but they rarely include the cost of keeping the printer running. If you print often, ink will probably cost more over a year than the printer itself.

The good news is that a few quick checks can protect you. Let’s start with the five hidden costs.

## **5 Hidden Ink Costs in Black Friday Printer Deals**

### **1. Starter Cartridges That Run Out Fast**

Many low-priced printers ship with “starter” or “setup” cartridges. These hold less ink than regular cartridges, so they may run dry within a few weeks of normal use.

**What to do:** Budget for a full set of replacement cartridges right away, and add that cost to the printer’s price before deciding if it’s a good deal.

### **2. Printers That Only Accept Original Ink**

Some printers are set up to work only with original brand cartridges. For example, many newer HP models offer HP+ during setup, and once it’s activated, only original HP ink works for the life of the printer. That means no cheaper compatible or remanufactured options. Our guide to HP+ Instant Ink printers explains which models are affected.

**What to do:** Check the product page for phrases like “original ink only” or “HP+.” On many models, you can skip HP+ during setup, so read every screen.

### **3. Subscription Fees**

Some Black Friday printer offers include a free trial of an ink plan. After the trial, you pay a monthly fee based on the pages you print, and the cartridges may stop working if you cancel. A [printer ink subscription](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/printer-ink-subscription-or-buy-ink-cartridges-which-saves-more.html) can make sense for light printers, but it isn’t always the cheapest option.

**What to do:** Mark the trial’s end date on your calendar, and compare the monthly fee against buying cartridges. Our guide on whether a printer ink subscription or buying cartridges saves more can help you decide.

### **4. Expensive Replacement Cartridges**

Two printers with the same price can have very different ink costs. Some models use cartridges that cost two or three times more than others.

**What to do:** Look up the replacement cartridge price before you buy. Search the cartridge number for the model you’re considering and compare it with other printers.

### **5. Low Page Yield**

“Page yield” is how many pages a cartridge can print. Standard cartridges often print about half as many pages as high-yield (XL) versions. For example, a standard HP 67 black cartridge prints around 120 pages, while the XL version prints around 240.

**What to do:** Compare cost per page, not cartridge price alone. A cheaper cartridge that prints fewer pages can cost more in the end.

## **How to Shop Black Friday Printer Sales Without Getting Trapped**

Before you click “Buy” during Black Friday printer sales, run through these three quick checks:

1. **Find the cartridge numbers.** Most product pages list them under “compatible ink” or “specifications.”
2. **Check the price of the cartridges.** Look at both standard and high-yield versions, and note how many pages each prints.
3. **Do the math.** Divide the cartridge price by its page yield. For example, a $20 cartridge that prints 200 pages costs 10 cents per page.

Then ask yourself how many pages you print each month. A student who prints a few pages a week needs very different ink than a home office printing hundreds.

Printer ink Black Friday sales can save you money, but you don’t have to wait for a sale to pay less for ink.

* **Buy high-yield cartridges.** They usually cost less per page.

For example, explore the [best third party ink cartridges HP printers](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/best-third-party-ink-cartridges-for-hp-printers-a-buyers-guide.html) can use, and confirm your model supports them first.
* **Skip extras you don’t need,** such as add-on plans and bundles you won’t use.

## **Quick Checklist Before You Buy**

Before you jump on Black Friday printer deals, here’s what to check first:

* Does the printer include full-size cartridges or just starter ones?
* Is the printer tied to original ink only?
* If there’s a free trial, when does the monthly fee begin?
* What do replacement cartridges cost, and how many pages do they print?
* Can I use third-party cartridges if I want to save more?

## **Final Thoughts**

Black Friday printer deals can be great when you know what to look for. Before you buy, check for starter cartridges, original-ink-only programs, subscription fees, expensive replacements, and low page yield. A little math during Black Friday printer sales can save you far more than any discount on the printer itself. Pick a printer you can afford to keep running, and the savings will last long after the sale ends.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. Are Black Friday printer deals really worth it?**

They can be, but only if you check the ink costs. A discounted printer with expensive cartridges can cost more over time than a pricier model with cheap ink.

### **2. When do Black Friday printer offers start?**

Many retailers begin sales before Black Friday itself and continue through Cyber Monday. Prices can change quickly, so check the exact model you want in the days beforehand.

### **3. Is it better to buy a printer on Black Friday or Cyber Monday?**

Both can have good prices. Choose the model first, then track its price over several days so you know what a real discount looks like.

### **4. Can I use cheaper cartridges in a printer I buy on sale?**

It depends on the printer. Many models accept compatible or remanufactured cartridges, but printers with HP+ activated accept original ink only. Check the product page before you buy.

### **5. Does the sale price include ink?**

Often not. Many sale printers include only starter cartridges, so plan to buy replacements soon.
