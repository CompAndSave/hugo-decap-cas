If you only print a few pages every week—or sometimes go weeks without printing—you may have wondered if there is a printer that ink doesn't dry out in between uses. Dried ink and clogged printheads can be frustrating, especially when you need an important document and your printer suddenly produces faded or streaky pages. But is there really a printer that doesn't dry out?

The good news is that some printers are better suited to occasional printing than others. The key is understanding how different printer types handle ink or toner and choosing one that matches how often you actually print.

In this guide, we'll explain which printer is best for infrequent printing, what to look for before buying, and simple ways to prevent dried ink and other common printing problems.

## **What Causes Printer Ink to Dry Out?**

Inkjet printers use liquid ink that passes through tiny openings to put text and images onto paper. When a printer sits unused for a long time, some of that ink can dry inside the cartridge, nozzles, or printhead.

This can result in:

* Faded or incomplete text
* Missing lines or colors
* Streaky prints
* Clogged printheads
* Wasted ink from repeated cleaning cycles
* Replacing cartridges sooner than expected

This doesn't mean every inkjet printer will dry out quickly. How fast ink dries depends on factors such as how often you print, the printer design, the type of ink, and where the printer is stored.

If you're wondering **[what is a printhead](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/what-is-a-printhead-troubleshooting-clogs-and-more-2025.html)**, it is the part of an inkjet printer that places tiny drops of ink onto the paper. Keeping it from becoming clogged is important for maintaining good print quality.

## **Is There a Printer That Ink Doesn't Dry Out?**

Technically, there isn't a traditional inkjet printer with ink that can never dry out. Liquid ink can eventually dry when exposed to air or left unused for extended periods.

However, some printers are less troublesome for infrequent printing than others.

For many people who print only occasionally, a laser printer can be a better choice. Instead of liquid ink, laser printers use toner, a dry powder that doesn't dry out in the same way ink does.

This is why someone searching for a printer that ink doesn't dry out may actually be better served by considering a laser printer rather than looking exclusively at inkjet models.

### **What About Ink Tank Printers?**

Ink tank printers are popular because they can offer a low cost per page and hold a large amount of ink. But they still use liquid ink, so they aren't completely immune to drying.

So, **[do ink tank printers dry out](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/do-ink-tank-printers-dry-out-what-every-buyer-should-know.html)**? Yes, they can, particularly when they aren't used for long periods. However, proper maintenance and occasional printing can help keep the ink flowing.

If you're considering an ink tank model, it's worth learning more about ink tanks before deciding whether one is right for your printing habits.

## **Laser vs. Inkjet: Which Is Better for Infrequent Printing?**

When choosing a printer with ink that doesn't dry out, the biggest decision may come down to whether you need an inkjet or laser printer.

### **Laser Printers**

Laser printers are often a good choice for people who don't print regularly. Their toner doesn't dry out like liquid ink, which means the printer can sit unused for longer without the same risk of dried ink clogging the printhead.

A laser printer may be a good fit if you:

* Print mostly documents
* Go weeks or months without printing
* Want a printer that's ready when you need it
* Prefer sharp text
* Don't regularly print high-quality photos

For someone looking for a printer that won’t dry out, a laser model is one of the closest practical options.

However, laser printers aren't perfect for everyone. If you regularly print colorful photos or need excellent photo quality, an inkjet printer may still be the better choice.

### **Inkjet Printers**

Inkjet printers are versatile and can be excellent for home printing. They can handle documents, graphics, and photos, and many models are affordable to purchase.

The downside is that liquid ink can dry when the printer isn't used regularly. If you choose an inkjet printer, occasional use and proper maintenance become more important.

Before making a decision, consider [inkjet vs. laser printing costs](https://www.compandsave.com/blog/posts/inkjet-vs-laser-ink-cost-which-printing-option-saves-you-more.html) to see which type makes more sense for your printing needs and budget.

## **Best Printer That Ink Doesn't Dry Out for Occasional Printing**

If you're searching for the best printer that ink doesn't dry out, don't focus only on the cartridge. Think about how you actually use your printer.

Here are some situations to consider:

### **Choose a Laser Printer for Very Infrequent Printing**

If you print only once or twice a month, a laser printer can be a convenient option because toner doesn't have the same drying problem as liquid ink.

This makes laser printers particularly appealing for:

* Occasional home office use
* Students who print assignments from time to time
* Families that mainly print school forms and documents
* Small businesses with low-volume document printing

### **Choose an Inkjet for Photos and Color**

If you frequently print photos, colorful graphics, or creative projects, an inkjet may be worth the extra maintenance.

You don't necessarily need to avoid inkjet printers just because you don't print every day. Instead, look for a model with features designed to help maintain print quality and follow the manufacturer's recommended maintenance routine.

### **Choose an Ink Tank Printer for Higher-Volume Printing**

Ink tank printers can be a good option if you print frequently enough to use the ink regularly. They typically hold much more ink than traditional cartridges and can have a low cost per page.

For someone who prints only a handful of pages every few months, though, the large ink capacity may not be the biggest advantage.

## **How to Keep Ink From Drying Out**

Even if you already own an inkjet printer, there are simple things you can do to reduce the chances of dried ink.

### **1. Print Something Regularly**

One of the easiest ways to keep an inkjet printer ready to use is to print a small document or color page regularly. You don't need to print a large batch—occasional use can help keep ink moving through the printhead.

### **2. Keep the Printer Properly Covered**

Dust and debris can contribute to printer problems. Keep your printer in a clean, reasonably dry area and use the manufacturer's recommended cover or storage method when applicable.

### **3. Don't Remove Cartridges Unnecessarily**

Cartridges are designed to remain installed in the printer. Frequently removing and reinstalling them can expose ink to air and may create additional opportunities for leaks or drying.

### **4. Use Your Printer's Cleaning Function**

If your prints start showing missing lines or faded colors, your printer may have a cleaning or printhead maintenance function. Use it according to the manufacturer's instructions.

### **5. Store Cartridges Properly**

If you have replacement cartridges waiting to be used, keep them sealed in their original packaging and follow the storage recommendations on the package.

## **What to Look for When Buying a Printer for Infrequent Use**

The right **printer that doesn't dry out** isn't necessarily the most expensive model. Before buying, consider these factors:

**Printing frequency:** How often will you really use it? If you print only a few times a month, prioritize reliability and low maintenance.

**Printer type:** Consider laser if you primarily print documents and want to avoid liquid ink drying. Consider inkjet if you need versatile color and photo printing.

**Running costs:** Don't look only at the printer's purchase price. Check cartridge or toner costs and estimated page yield.

**Maintenance requirements:** A printer that requires frequent cleaning may not be ideal for someone who rarely prints.

**Your typical print jobs:** Think about whether you mostly print text, schoolwork, business documents, photos, or a combination of these.

## **So, What Is the Best Printer for Infrequent Printing?**

For people who print very occasionally and mainly need documents, a **laser printer is often the most practical choice**. Since toner is a dry powder, it doesn't have the same drying and clogging concerns associated with liquid ink.

An inkjet can still be a good choice if you need color and photo printing. Just remember that occasional maintenance and periodic printing can help prevent dried ink and clogged nozzles.

If you're looking for a printer that ink doesn't dry out, the most important thing to remember is that no liquid-ink printer can guarantee that its ink will never dry. Instead, choose a printer that fits your printing habits and understand what maintenance it requires.

## **Conclusion**

Finding a printer that ink doesn't dry out is less about finding a completely maintenance-free inkjet and more about choosing the right printer technology for your printing habits. If you print very rarely and mostly need documents, a laser printer may be the best choice because toner doesn't dry out like liquid ink.

If you prefer an inkjet for color or photo printing, regular use and proper maintenance can help reduce dried ink and clogged printheads. Ultimately, the best printer that doesn't dry out is one that matches how often you print, what you print, and how much maintenance you're willing to do.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. Is there really a printer that doesn't dry out?**

There isn't a traditional inkjet printer that doesn't dry out completely because liquid ink can eventually dry when left unused. Laser printers are generally a better option if your main concern is avoiding the drying problems associated with liquid ink.

### **2. What is the best printer that ink doesn't dry out for occasional use?**

For people who mainly print documents and may leave their printer unused for weeks, a laser printer is often a strong choice. Its toner doesn't dry out like liquid ink, making it suitable for infrequent printing.

### **3. Do ink tank printers dry out if not used?**

Yes. Ink tank printers use liquid ink, so the ink can still dry or clog if the printer remains unused for a long time. Regular use and proper maintenance can help reduce the risk.

### **4. How often should I use an inkjet printer to prevent drying?**

There isn't one universal schedule for every printer. If you own an inkjet printer, occasional printing can help keep the ink flowing. Follow the maintenance recommendations provided for your specific model.

### **5. Can I fix dried ink instead of replacing my printer?**

Sometimes. If the problem is caused by a clogged printhead or nozzle, cleaning or running the printer's built-in maintenance routine may restore print quality.
